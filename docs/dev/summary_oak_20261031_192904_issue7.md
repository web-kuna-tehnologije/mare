# Mare Liberum - summary, worktree oak, 2026-10-31 late (0.22.10: issue7 + the trailer)

## issue7, item by item

**More fire, greyer smoke.** The flash is bigger and longer, two tongues of flame run out ahead
of each gun (one low along the water), twice the burning wad; the smoke shades come down again
(core 0.72, plume 0.56, haze 0.52).

**Guns not all at once.** Every gun is queued with its own random delay - a fifth go off
together, the rest are strung out over up to ~1.7 s on a big side - so a broadside is a ripple
of flashes and separate clouds at different ages along the hull.

**The foam, for real this time.** `assets/ships/foam/new` holds two foam masks flattened onto
a grey checkerboard; `assets/ships/FOAM` a tileable foam photograph and a labelled sheet.
`tools/foam_tiles.py` is rewritten for them: the masks are thresholded above the checker's
two greys (found as the two histogram peaks) so the checker vanishes and the foam stays; the
photograph is gently high-passed; the sheet's streak panel is cut out. The wake and the hull
break read the masks; the whitecaps and the collapsing crests read the photograph at two
scales, and the crest band is wider and heavier.

**Sails tied to the ship.** The roll and pitch were applied to every part about its own
origin, so a sail high over the deck rolled about its own foot while the rail under it swung
sideways. All three vertex stages (mesh, depth, cordage) now rotate the whole ship about one
pivot at the hull's waterline; masts, yards, canvas and oars sweep the same arc as the hull.

**The "weird texture over the ship".** The stipple in the screenshot was the 0.22.9
near-camera dissolve biting on a hull the camera was looking at. The band is tighter (1.2
units over the near plane, was 3) and exists only in the staged scenes.

**Sharper tessellation.** Sea 16 -> 24 subdivisions per patch edge, terrain 32 -> 40 with
finer cells.

## The trailer

- `MARE_RECORD=dir,fps,seconds[,warmup]` (main.cc): the frame clock is locked to 1/fps, so the
  film runs at true speed however long a frame takes to render; every frame after the warm-up
  is written as a BMP before the UI.
- `MARE_BATTLE_SHIPS=44` stages a 44-ship exercise; the formation's straight leg grows with
  the line so the biggest hull keeps a gap of its own length. The lines now start *engaged*
  (leaders abreast of the centre) instead of four minutes apart.
- `MARE_WEATHER=fog`: a sea fog you can still see a battle through (full `mist` whited the
  first take out).
- `detailview_trailer`: a 45 s keyed camera - wide over the lanes, down them, close on a
  broadside each side, up over the fleet, away into the fog - on the recorder's clock.
- `tools/blender_encode_mp4.py`: Blender's bundled ffmpeg encodes the BMP sequence to H.264,
  so no ffmpeg install is needed. `tools-bat\trailer.bat [seconds] [fps]` does the whole take.
- Output: `C:\projects\mare_oak_20261031_202444\trailer\mare_battle_trailer.mp4`
  (2700 frames, 1600x900, 60 fps, 45 s, H.264 ~50 MB) with the BMP frames beside it. Two
  frames are in `docs/screenshots/issue7_oak_trailer_*.png`.
- Blender 5.2 API notes baked into the script: the sequencer collection is `strips` (an empty
  collection is falsy, so test the attribute name), and `FFMPEG` is only a valid file format
  once `image_settings.media_type = 'VIDEO'`.
