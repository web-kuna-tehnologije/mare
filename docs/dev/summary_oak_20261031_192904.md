## issue6

**The water a ship leaves.** `gfx/wake.cc` is a 1024px map of the sea round the staged battle
that every ship paints her churn into each frame and which fades over ~50 s. Because it is
written in the world and not attached to the hull, a ship that wears round leaves her wake
where she actually was. The sea's vertex stages read it as water already broken, the fragment
stage as foam.

**The foam tiles.** `tools/foam_tiles.py`. What is in `assets/ships/FOAM` is two *sailcloth*
sheets - the same hessian the sail tiles came from. Their tears are the right structure, so
the tool keeps only those: high-pass, de-stripe (subtracting per-column and per-row means,
without which the weave and seams tiled across the sea as visible quilting - that was the
first attempt and it looked like a quilt), threshold to the brightest quarter, blur.

**The broadside on the water.** The flat white discs are gone. One ring per broadside is
displaced and whitened in the sea's own surface, heavily weighted to the side she fired, with
a hole over the water under the guns. The screen-space air shock is a third as strong.

**The smoke** is grey rather than white, four balls a layer instead of three, each thrown off
the gun's line, with the silhouette warped by a second coarse noise so no puff is a circle.

**The spritsail** was cut tall and narrow with its head above the bowsprit and its foot in the
sea - the sliver the screenshot circles. It is now a shallow square sail, wider than deep,
bent under the bowsprit.

**The near-camera blackout** is fixed: geometry within ~1.15x the near plane is dropped and
the band above it dissolves, in the mesh, cloth and cordage pipelines.

**A Battle panel** with a wind-arrows button (and stop/run, the speeds, Leave).

## Physics foundation (P1.3 items 1-6)

`sim/naval_physics.h/.cc`, with `game/tests/physics_test.cpp`:

1. 120 Hz fixed step, accumulator, 8 steps a frame max.
2. The tactical sub-step: drive, drag, integrate, ship contacts, coast - that order, always.
3. Interpolated rigid-body transforms (previous pose + leftover accumulator); longitudinal and
   lateral drag, so a hull carves a turn; helm authority from her way; heel from the turn,
   which scales the renderer's roll.
4. Compound hulls: three discs down the keel, radii from the beam.
5. Ship-to-ship: broadphase, disc pairs, inverse-mass correction, restitution, friction, yaw.
6. Ship-to-coast: the staged shore profile as a signed distance; too little water under a
   disc and she grounds, is pushed seaward and slewed.

The staged battle runs on it - the formation only asks for a course and a speed now.
